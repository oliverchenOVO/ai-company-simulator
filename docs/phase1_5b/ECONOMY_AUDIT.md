# Early economy and hiring value

Starting capital500k, founder salaries25k/40k/30k, operating cost10k monthly, initial payroll95k and runway4.762months remain unchanged. Acquisition trials still occur weekly, at most one new contract per week with probability capped0.9; startup hiring accelerates launch without guaranteeing enough booked revenue before salary obligations. Payroll and revenue accrue exact active days and are recognized at calendar month close. CurrentMRR is not historical earned cash. Contract values and churn equations are unchanged (see previous phase1_5/ECONOMY_AUDIT.md). The only new parameters are independent role-relative expectation and acceptance tolerance, including consent for non-founder salary cuts.

OneEngineer accelerates startup launch14–20days here, but90-day incremental cash is negative inall5 samples. Three hires accelerate more but consume substantially more cash. In established contexts oneSales produces positive365-day incremental cash inbenchmark-002(+133,189.72),010(+130,662.55),050(+26,416.02), negative in001/003. These amounts already include actual incremental payroll/revenue, and show realized cash payback in those branches; they are not a universal expectedROI estimate. Higher terminalMRR alone would not establish return. There is no universal claim hiring is balanced.

The economy remains narrow in early hiring window: starting runway, payroll cadence and capped acquisitions dominate. However, decisions matter, multiple prudent survival paths exist, and hiring is useful in plausible funded-by-existing-business conditions. No change to capital/revenue/product economics is justified by compensation repair alone. Avoiding all hiring forever is not universally superior in matched established-company examples. Human playtest is still needed to understand perceived fairness and whether these conditions are discoverable.
