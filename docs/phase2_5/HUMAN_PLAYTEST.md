## Participant instruction (share this section)

Open [FOUNDRY](https://foundry-company-simulator.oliverchenovo.chatgpt.site/) in your own browser profile. Start a Garage Startup with the supplied seed and default funds. Run your company as you see fit for 30–45 real minutes or 12–24 simulated months, whichever comes first. Explore the controls; there is no prescribed optimal strategy. You can advance time, hire, change pay, manage teams and promotions, inspect finance/product decisions, and inspect recorded events. Stop if your company fails; failure is useful evidence too.

Before beginning, export any company you want to keep: creating another company replaces the current automatic slot. Refreshing preserves saves in that browser profile; there is no cross-device account sync. At the end, save, refresh and check that your company returns. Export the final save only if you consent to facilitator analysis; use fictional company/employee names and include no personal information.

## Facilitator instructions

Recruit 3–5 independent participants, or the maximum available. Assign anonymous IDs A–E. Offer only basic control help when asked; do not teach Conservative, salary optimization or the meaning of hidden thresholds. Use fixed seeds `human-validation-A` through `human-validation-E`, or record the exact participant-chosen seed. Keep default cash and v3 rules. Explain that no personal details are needed. Ask permission before recording comments; do not record a screen containing unrelated apps or account information.

Record elapsed real time, simulated dates, the first danger recognized, actual decisions, and where the participant pauses or misinterprets a message. Keep participant feedback and consented saves outside Git, e.g. a private local folder chosen by the facilitator. Do not upload or send feedback to others without authorization. Commit only anonymized summaries checked against the participant's actual words.

An economic failure before a career issue appears is a valid observation, not a reason to secretly add money or coach a winning strategy. For follow-up questions about manager/promotion tradeoffs, mark whether understanding was spontaneous or arose after facilitator prompting. Never substitute a controlled automated case for independent gameplay.

## Post-play questions

1. What was the most important problem in your company?
2. When did you first realize your company was in danger?
3. Did you understand why specific employees were unhappy?
4. Did you understand what managers actually changed?
5. Did you understand when a manager was overloaded?
6. Did team organization feel meaningful?
7. Did promotions feel important?
8. Did you ever delay or avoid a promotion because of tradeoffs?
9. Did any employee feel memorable?
10. Did any event feel arbitrary?
11. If an employee became a retention concern, did you understand why?
12. What decision would you change if replaying the same company?
13. Did the game feel too easy, too hard or unclear?
14. Did you understand why your company succeeded or failed?

Preserve qualitative responses. Do not convert these into a score or infer stronger claims than the participant made.
