# Retention changes and compatibility decision

## Simulation equations

**No retention equation or parameter was changed. Simulation remains v3; v4 was not introduced.** Released v1/v2/v3 systems, update order, RNG, compensation guards and goldens remain untouched. App 0.2.1 changes explanations and developer tooling only; app version and save-envelope metadata are independent from world behavior.

Problem: normal Phase 2 policies showed no departures, and career warnings could outlive meaningful progression. Evidence: this phase measures the pipeline, controlled mixed-risk states, warning durations, matched interventions and longer survival runs; see RETENTION_AUDIT.md. A resignation quota or arbitrary weekly probability increase would not address the upstream failure to enter search. Human participants are still unavailable. The requested scope is diagnostics and a test package first, so this phase documents calibration candidates without retroactively changing released worlds.

Old behavior retained: career frustration is bounded, job satisfaction and support can keep intent below search even with an unresolved goal. Departures are possible under sustained compounded pressure. Completed primary goals do not renew automatically. These are measured limitations, not implicit fixes.

Expected and measured simulation effect of this release: **none**. Independent replay/restore checks and unchanged old goldens verify preservation. Any subsequent evidence-backed retention calibration must add v4 and separate goldens/fixtures, rather than edit v3 or rescale probability to meet a quota.

## Explanation changes (no world mutation)

| Problem / evidence | Old display | Change / expected effect | Regression and compatibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generic concern can occur with low stress and unmet career expectations; the event has no extra causal factors | Non-compensation concern asserted a workload problem | Ask players to compare workload, career, manager and compensation records; explicitly avoid asserting the reason from this event alone | New rendered concern/intervention tests; event type, payload, causal data, world hash unchanged |
| Different tracks have real output/responsibility consequences | Shared static management paragraph | Selection-specific promotion explanation: manager track consumes individual time but creates neither skill nor reports automatically; specialist track may not fulfill a leadership goal | Desktop/mobile organization flow retained; new track-toggle assertions; no weights shown |
| Why panel showed raw organization factor keys such as management-support | Some factor headings remained technical keys | Translate known factors into Chinese while retaining actual positive cause evidence and links | New career/departure UI tests, existing zero-weight filtering retained; historical replay unchanged |

These improvements have automated interaction coverage. They have not been declared understood by humans. Gradual recovery and subsequent history remain actual simulation outcomes; no UI-only risk escalation or instant magical recovery was added.

## Deferred candidates

Review career-warning persistence and the missing connection between long-lived unmet expectations and search under otherwise moderate conditions. Review completed-goal inertness as a potential minimal v4 mechanism or later Phase 3 scope. First obtain human interpretation evidence, then test a falsifiable proposed rule against strong retention, moderate compounding, early warning and costly intervention scenarios. Do not broadly rebalance cash, customer acquisition, product speed or salary anchors.

## Controlled moderate-input limitation

The combined-moderate recipe uses actual legal pay, workload and manager commands; sampled focal stress never exceeds its initial 15 and burnout remains low. It therefore tests compounded moderate inputs, but does not demonstrate four sustained moderate psychological risks. A bounded workload experiment remains necessary before claiming that acceptance condition. Do not inject hidden state or tune v3 to meet a departure quota.
